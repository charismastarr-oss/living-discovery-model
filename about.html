About

Charisma Starr

25+ years in legal technology and eDiscovery, spent building the tools and standards this framework now asks the field to rethink.

Charisma Starr has spent more than twenty five years in legal technology, eDiscovery, and AI strategy, including as Head of Legal Technology and eDiscovery at Exelon, a Fortune 89 company, as a founding member of Reveal, and as head of Kirkland & Ellis's Litigation Support department in Chicago. She has advised legal technology companies including Microsoft, Relativity, and Exterro, and has spoken at more than seventy industry events on the intersection of AI, neurodiversity, and the future of legal work.

She led development work on EDRM 2.0, the first substantive update to the Electronic Discovery Reference Model since it incorporated the Information Governance Reference Model, before stepping away. EDRM 2.0 extends analysis across the discovery workflow and grounds it in a broader governance lifecycle, real improvements, but it keeps the nine-phase structure the original model was built around in 2005. The Living Discovery Model started from a different question: if a governed LLM can query a corporation's own data in place, conversationally, continuously, which of those nine phases were ever really separate events, and which were artifacts of data having to move before anyone could look at it.

Drawing on feedback from her EDRM 2.0 discussions, Charisma hopes this framework supports software companies and thought leaders shaping the future of eDiscovery. As her views continue to evolve, she wholeheartedly welcomes feedback and ideas, reach her on LinkedIn.

Charisma writes and speaks publicly as one of the few openly AuDHD women in the legal field, and centers neurodiversity, AI, and the future of legal work as the throughline of her independent practice.
